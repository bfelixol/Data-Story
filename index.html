Arizona Population Data Story

Arizona Is Growing — But Not Everywhere

Arizona's population is projected to continue growing through 2060, but that growth will not be evenly distributed across the state. Population projections from the Arizona Office of Economic Opportunity show especially strong growth in Maricopa and Pinal counties, while several rural counties are expected to lose population. These differences matter because population change can affect housing, transportation, infrastructure, schools, and other community resources. Looking at county-level projections reveals that Arizona's future growth is not simply a statewide trend, but a geographic one.

D1 · Story Frame

One state, different directions

Arizona is projected to add more than two million residents between 2025 and 2060. However, the statewide total hides major differences between counties. The central question of this story is: Where is Arizona's future population growth expected to occur, and which counties are projected to move in the opposite direction?

Methods and source: This story uses the Arizona Office of Economic Opportunity's 2025–2060 population projections. County projections are compared to show differences in expected population change across Arizona. Any calculations or transformations used in later figures and tables are documented with those elements.

Data Source

Population data comes from the Arizona Office of Economic Opportunity's Arizona State and County Population Projections: 2025–2060, Medium Series.

Data Transformation

Numeric population change was calculated by subtracting the 2025 population from the projected 2060 population. Percent change was calculated by dividing the numeric change by the 2025 population and multiplying by 100. Percentages were rounded to one decimal place. Values shown in the SVG were rounded to millions for readability.
